# The *Tag along* Playbook

Everything we know about running a *Tag along*, on one page: [tags](#so-tags), [the program](#program), [logistics](#logistics), [outreach](#outreach), and [tips](#tips). It's all [CC-BY-4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), so take what's useful and change the rest. If something's missing, [tell us](/contact/) and we'll add it.

## What you need to know

**A tag is the last few bars of a barbershop song**, the big ending, usually built around one long, gloriously held "money chord." You can teach one by ear in ten to twenty minutes to people who have never sung barbershop and don't read music. That's why tags work so well for a drop-in night. Nobody has to prepare, and if someone arrives late, they've only missed one tag.

**The one thing you really need is a tag teacher**, someone who can sing every part of a tag and then hold their own while the room learns the others. If that's not you yet, that's fine! A learning track or a sing-along video can teach the parts for you (see [how to teach a tag](#how-to-teach-a-tag)), and you'll get better at it every time you run an evening.

**Things you don't need:** an existing chorus or organization, funding, a fancy venue, experienced singers, or a minimum crowd. Four people is a quartet. Eight people is two. The four parts are **tenor, lead, bari, and bass**, and any mix of voices can sing them. Most tags exist in men's, women's, and mixed voicings, and people can sing any part in whatever octave works. Say "all voices welcome" in your invitation and mean it.

## What you need to prepare

Your first *Tag along* takes about two or three hours of preparation:

<ul class="checklist">
<li><strong>Date and venue</strong> booked (see <a href="#venue-requirements">venue requirements</a>). A weekday evening works well.</li>
<li><strong>Two or three tags</strong> chosen (see <a href="#good-first-tags">good first tags</a>), with sheet music printed, around ten copies each. You'll teach by ear, but many people feel safer with the paper in hand.</li>
<li><strong>A way to give pitches:</strong> pitch pipe, toy piano, or a pitch pipe app on your phone.</li>
<li><strong>Invitation</strong> sent two to three weeks ahead (steal our <a href="/invitation/">sample invitation</a>), with a reminder two days before.</li>
<li><strong>Name tags and a marker.</strong> Cheap, and a room of strangers starts using each other's names.</li>
<li><strong>Roles for the evening:</strong> someone teaches, someone greets people at the door. It can be the same heroic person, but it's nicer if it isn't.</li>
<li><strong>A decision about money:</strong> free, donations, or a small cover for the venue (see <a href="#budget-and-fundraising">budget</a>).</li>
<li><strong>A <a href="/code-of-conduct/">code of conduct</a>.</strong> Adopt ours if you like, link it in the invitation, and name a contact person.</li>
</ul>

Nice to have: snacks, water, and a phone for group photos (ask before posting them).

## So... tags

### Where to find tags

- **[barbershoptags.com](https://www.barbershoptags.com)** is the world's largest tag library: over 7,000 free tags with sheet music, per-part learning tracks (with an on-page mixer), and videos. It's the one everybody uses, though it's not great on a phone.
- **The Classic Tags books** collect 125 standard tags, put together by arranger David Wright and friends, free as PDFs from the Barbershop Harmony Society: [men's/TTBB voicing](https://www.barbershop.org/files/documents/tags/classic_tags.pdf) and [women's/SSAA voicing](https://www.barbershoptags.com/ClassicTags-Women.pdf). barbershoptags.com has the same tags with learning tracks.
- **The [BHS free tags page](https://www.barbershop.org/music/free-music-and-tags/barbershop-tags)** has standard tags in TTBB, SSAA, *and* SATB voicings, plus beginner-friendly "tags featuring a post." The SATB ones are handy for mixed groups.
- **[dnstags.com](https://www.dnstags.com/)** is Darwin Scheel's catalog of 1,450+ original tags. You can filter by difficulty, voicing, and which part gets the spotlight.

### How to teach a tag

What we've settled on after a lot of evenings:

1. **Perform it first.** Sing the tag with a quartet, or play a video or learning track. People learn faster when they know what they're building.
2. **Teach every part to everyone.** Go phrase by phrase, call and response, with the *whole room* singing each part. People find out which part suits them, and later they can help out on other parts. A common order is bass (the foundation), then lead (the melody), then tenor, then bari. Save the weird one for last, and tell the baris you love them.
3. **Stack the parts.** Basses and leads together, then add tenors, then baris. If a chord crunches, isolate the two parts that rub and sing them as a duet. This is some of the best ear training ;)
4. **Sing it down two or three times**, then hold the last chord longer than is reasonable. That ring is what everyone came for.
5. **Don't over-explain.** No theory lecture, no vowel clinic on night one. Fix things by singing them again, not by talking about them.

More from people who've taught far more tags than we have:

- [Barbershop Tag Teaching Guidelines](https://www.barbershoptags.com/Barbershop_Tag_Teaching_Guidelines.pdf) (PDF), Darwin Scheel's step-by-step handout.
- The BHS's [Learn a barbershop tag!](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLE3D51D3A12D7C498) playlist teaches tags part by part. Watch it to see how it's done, or play it in the room when there's no teacher.
- The [Barbershop Tag-Along](https://www.youtube.com/c/BarbershopTagAlong) YouTube channel posts a new sing-along tag video every week. No relation to us, but great name.
- [Intro to Barbershop: casual harmony](https://www.barbershop.org/intro-to-barbershop-have-fun-singing-casual-harmony), the BHS on tag culture and etiquette.

### Good first tags

A good first tag is short (about eight bars), moves mostly stepwise in every part, has few words, and lands on one obvious money chord. Save the key changes and the six-flat David Wright masterpieces for later in the night!

**Start the evening with these.** All are free and have learning tracks.

- The [Easy Tags collection](https://www.barbershoptags.com/TagsList?Collection=easytags&Sortby=Classic) on barbershoptags.com has 51 beginner tags. Our favorites are [Smile (easy version)](https://www.barbershoptags.com/tag-1053-Smile), [Sunshine](https://www.barbershoptags.com/tag-1067-Sunshine), and [By and By](https://www.barbershoptags.com/tag-1210-By-and-By).
- The **"hanger" tags** on the [BHS free tags page](https://www.barbershop.org/music/free-music-and-tags/barbershop-tags), which the BHS labels "great for beginners." Each one gives a different voice part a long held note to show off (the "post"), so every part gets a turn to be the star.
- [Smile](https://www.barbershoptags.com/tag-1-Smile) and [I Love to Sing 'Em](https://www.barbershoptags.com/tag-2-I-Love-to-Sing-'Em), numbers 1 and 2 in the Classic Tags book for a reason.

**Crowd-pleasers once the room is warm.** These are the most downloaded tags on barbershoptags.com: [Lost](https://www.barbershoptags.com/tag-1809-Lost-(52Eighty-Version)), [Last Night Was the End of the World](https://www.barbershoptags.com/tag-36-Last-Night-Was-The-End-Of-The-World), [Sleepytime Down South](https://www.barbershoptags.com/tag-4-Sleepytime-Down-South), [Lone Prairie](https://www.barbershoptags.com/tag-68-Lone-Prairie), and [The Sunshine of Your Smile](https://www.barbershoptags.com/tag-19-The-Sunshine-of-Your-Smile). Someone in the room probably knows them already. Promote that person to tag teacher on the spot.

### Good first songs

Once your regulars can't get enough of tags, give them whole songs. The **Barberpole Cat Program** ("polecats") is the Barbershop Harmony Society's shared songbook. Barbershoppers all over the world learn the same twelve songs, so two people who have never met can sing a whole song together. The [Volume 1 songbook](https://shop.barbershop.org/barberpole-cat-songbook-vol-i-download/) is $8 as a download from the BHS shop, and BHS's [Harmonize at Home page](https://www.barbershop.org/harmonize-at-home-sing-the-polecats) streams **free part-predominant learning tracks** for all twelve songs. When your group has eaten through Volume 1, a [Volume 2](https://shop.barbershop.org/barberpole-cat-songbook-vol-ii-print/) with twelve more songs (including "Goodbye, My Coney Island Baby" and "Over the Rainbow") is waiting.

## Program

### Sample 2-hour+ program

| When | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 5 min | Welcome | Who you are, how the evening works, code of conduct in one sentence |
| 10 min | Introductions | Keep it short if the group is big |
| 5 min | Voice part assignments | If need be; people can switch anytime |
| 20 min | Teach first tag + **group quartetting** | |
| 20 min | Teach second tag + **group quartetting** | Ask what people noticed when singing vs. listening |
| 10 min | Break | Water, snacks, chatter |
| 40–60 min | Free tag singing | Let a few experienced singers teach their favorites; this part can run long |
| 10 min | Last tag of the night | Sing in group again, thank everyone, announce the next date |

### Group quartetting

Group quartetting is a format we came up with for *Tag along*. We wanted newcomers to try quartet singing, one voice on each part, with some guidance.

We ask a first group with all four voice parts to stand in box formation. They each identify their voice part (lead, tenor, bari, bass). Everyone else lines up behind the singer whose part they want to sing. The quartet in the middle sings the tag everyone just learned; when they're done, the next people in line can "tag out" the singers in the middle. Not everyone needs to be tagged out every round, and you can switch voice parts whenever you like.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/img/quartet-formation.png" alt="Diagram of group quartetting: four singers stand in a box formation facing each other, with a line of waiting singers behind each of the four positions." width="321" height="240">
  <figcaption>Group quartetting: a quartet in the middle, the next singers lined up behind each part.</figcaption>
</figure>

People get to sing their part alone, as they would in a quartet. The singers waiting behind hear both their own part and the whole quartet up close, which is excellent ear training. Because the middle keeps changing, everyone sings in many different combinations over the evening. And nobody has to sing. Waiting your turn and listening is a fine way to spend a round, especially if you're new and don't know anyone yet.

### Variations

- **Polecat break.** Swap one tag-teaching slot for a [polecat](#good-first-songs) once your regulars know a few. A whole song is a nice change of pace.
- **Bring-a-tag night.** Experienced singers each teach their favorite tag in parallel small groups, then the groups perform for each other.
- **Guest teacher.** Visiting barbershoppers love being handed a room. Ask them a week ahead.
- **Themed nights.** Holiday tags, a night of one arranger's tags, or "tags we ruined last time."

## Logistics

### Venue requirements

**Must-have:**

- You can be **loud** without anyone minding. That rules out most cafés, and coworking spaces where people are still working at 8pm.
- **Free or nearly free.** Once there's a venue bill, someone has to collect money at the door, and it stops feeling like a drop-in.
- **Space to stand in a circle** and break into boxes of four; roughly 30 m² for 15 people.
- **Reachable by public transport** (if your city has it), and easy to find on first visit.
- **Acoustics that give a little back.** A slightly live room flatters chords and beginners; heavy carpet and curtains swallow them. (A church hall is great. A padded rehearsal cell is sad.)

**Should-have:**

- Chairs to rest between rounds, somewhere for coats and bags, toilets, drinking water.
- A **bookable recurring slot**. Same place, same weekday, and people start keeping that evening free.
- **Step-free access.** Aim for it; at minimum, state the access situation in your invitation so nobody travels across town to be surprised by a staircase.

**Nice-to-have:**

- Warm light (fluorescent tubes are the enemy of romance *and* overtones), a keyboard or piano, a table for sheet music and snacks, and a pub within walking distance for afterwards.

**Where to look:** community and cultural centers, church halls and parish rooms, university music departments, cooperative meeting rooms, offices after hours, the back room of a friendly pub on its slowest night, and in summer, a park (with a rain plan B).

### Budget and fundraising

A *Tag along* can run on no budget at all: free venue, donation box, done. Our real costs per event are printing (€5–15) plus one-off purchases (a pitch pipe for €15–30, name tags, markers). If you must pay for a venue:

- **Pay-what-you-can** with a suggested €3–5 covers most small venue fees. Announce once what the money covers, then stop talking about money. In our experience, a small cover also lowers the no-show rate.
- **Ask your national barbershop association** ([see below](#outreach)) about community or outreach grants; starting new groups is exactly what they want to fund.
- **Ask a local chorus to adopt the event**. It feeds them curious singers; their rehearsal venue is probably free at some hour.

### Invitations

Give your event a name and a face! You can also take our brand as-is or remix it.

Definitely steal our [sample invitation](/invitation/). Whatever you write, include: date, time, exact room, cost, "no experience needed, all voices welcome," what (not) to bring, the access situation, and a link to your [code of conduct](/code-of-conduct/). Announce about three weeks out, remind two days before. Don't ask people to RSVP. Drop-in is the whole idea. Only ask for sign-ups if the room really can't fit everyone.

## Outreach

How do you get people to come? Go where singers already are:

- **Raid the choirs (lovingly).** Your first attendees are already singing somewhere: pop and jazz choirs, church choirs, university ensembles, a cappella groups. Write to directors, visit rehearsals, bring flyers. "One evening, no commitment" is an easy yes for busy chorus singers.
- **Your national barbershop association** can announce you to members in your area and knows singers you don't. In Europe: [BinG!](https://barbershop-in-germany.de) (Germany), [SWABS](https://barbershopharmony.ch) (Switzerland), [BABS](https://www.singbarbershop.com) and [LABBS](https://www.labbs.org.uk) (UK), [Holland Harmony](https://hollandharmony.nl) (Netherlands), [IABS](https://www.irishbarbershop.com) (Ireland), [SNOBS](https://www.snobs.org) (Nordics). The [World Harmony Council directory](https://www.worldbarbershop.org/associations) lists every association worldwide.
- **Local event listings.** In Berlin we use [Gratis in Berlin](https://www.gratis-in-berlin.de) (free-events portal) and the [Chorverband Berlin](https://www.chorverband-berlin.de) network; Germany-wide there's [acappella-online.de](https://acappella-online.de) with an event calendar and singer classifieds. Your city has equivalents: free-event portals, choir association newsletters, "what's on" sites.
- **Flyers where singers go.** Music schools, sheet music shops, rehearsal venues, conservatory notice boards.
- **An Instagram account.** Short tag clips, 30 seconds or so, travel surprisingly far among singers. Post the ring, not the logistics.
- **A mailing list from day one.** Collect addresses on paper at every event, and send one email afterwards with a photo, a thank-you, and the next date.
- **Bring-a-friend** works better than everything above. Ask for it out loud at the end of every event.

## Tips

Here are some unsolicited bits of advice, in no particular order.

- **Whoever shows up is the right crowd.** Don't worry about whether there are too few or too many people, or whether they're too new or too experienced. Have a blast with the people who came, and make sure they want to come back.
- **Your job is permission-giving.** As facilitator, you set the temperature: sing out cheerfully, crack the chord yourself once, laugh about it, go again. The moment people see that mistakes are part of the fun, the room relaxes and the sound doubles.
- **Things we say when someone sings off key:** "Let's all take that phrase once more." "Basses, sing that with me." "Love that you went for it!" **Things we don't say:** anyone's name attached to a wrong note, "someone is flat," or any theory lecture. Fix by repetition, not diagnosis; if one singer keeps struggling, quietly go stand next to them and sing their part strongly.
- **Adopt a [code of conduct](/code-of-conduct/) before you need it.** Then actually mention it in your welcome: one sentence, who to talk to, done. It signals whom the event is for, and it makes the hard conversation easier if it ever comes.
- **Ask before you post.** Group photos: after asking the room. Videos of a specific quartet: ask those four people.
- **Book three dates ahead.** Leave a month without a date and people drift away. A fixed rhythm like "second and fourth Monday" keeps going through holidays, illness, and your own busy weeks.
- **End on a high.** Last tag: something the room already sings well. People remember the ending, so send them into the night still ringing.

## Where this can lead

A *Tag along* is worth running for its own sake, but things grow out of it. Four people keep ending up in the same box and decide to become a quartet. Regulars learn a few polecats, and now your group has songs everyone knows. In Berlin, *Tag along* led to [Capital Chords](https://capitalchords.de/), a mixed barbershop chorus that now runs the *Tag alongs* itself. If something like that happens to you, congratulations! Then please [update this playbook](/contact/) with what you learned, and send the elevator back down.
