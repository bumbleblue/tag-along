**Hello fellow barbershop-obsessed organizer!**

*Tag along* is a drop-in night of barbershop tag singing, and you're welcome to copy it. This site has almost everything you need to run one in your own town, for anyone who wants to sing, from the merely curious to the veteran poster.

The [Playbook](/playbook/) has the details: which tags to teach first, how to run the evening, where to find a room, and how to get singers through the door. If you'd rather see one in action first, come [tag along with us](/events/).

## Why are *Tag alongs* so great?

- Tags train your ear. A tag is short enough to sing ten times in a row, and it's mostly the big, ringing chords that barbershop is known for.
- Tags are a first taste of quartet singing. Chorus singers get to hold a part on their own, often for the first time. And since you sing with so many different people in one evening, you find out who you'd like to form a quartet with.
- You find out who in your city wants to sing barbershop, without asking anyone to commit to anything. If you'd like to start a barbershop chapter one day, that's your list of first members.
- Just have fun singing barbershop! No pressure to compete, learn music, or get dressed up. No pressure to come to every event, either. It's there whenever you're free and feel like singing.

## What's the *Tag along* story?

Eileen started *Tag along* in Berlin in 2019. The city was full of good singers, and plenty of them were curious about barbershop, but hardly anyone could promise to make a weekly chorus or quartet rehearsal. Eileen couldn't either. Andy was looking for barbershop singers at the time and turned out to be an excellent tag teacher, so the two of them started a drop-in evening: learn a tag, sing it with whoever turned up, come back whenever you can.

Eileen moved away for a few years. Andy kept going, founded [Capital Chords](https://capitalchords.de/), a mixed barbershop chorus, and still hosts a *Tag along* in Berlin every other week. This site is here so you can start one where you live.
